<p>According to market analysis, bitcoin traders are eyeing two key indicators, the funding and three-month annualised basis rates, indicating a potential upward price movement.</p>



<p>A crypto expert, Will Clemente, noted in a recent post on X, &#8220;Looks like we&#8217;re consolidating before the next leg up,&#8221; highlighting that both indicators have &#8220;cooled off&#8221; after briefly dipping into negative territory.</p>



<p>The funding rate, a significant indicator of overall trader sentiment, recently recovered from negative territory. This recovery suggests an increasing confidence among traders in Bitcoin&#8217;s price. Exchanges use the funding rate to balance traders entering long positions with those opting for short positions, mitigating the risk of overexposure.</p>



<p>According to open-source data, the open interest-weighted funding rate is 0.0091%, indicating a positive shift from a negative rate of -0.0050% on May 4.</p>



<p>&#8220;The Bitcoin funding rates remaining this low, while Bitcoin is bouncing makes me feel extremely bullish,&#8221; echoed pseudonymous crypto trader Mister Crypto to their 98,000 X followers.</p>



<p>However, liquidation data contradicts this bullish sentiment, suggesting that futures traders are still leaning bearish and anticipate a near-term price drop.</p>



<p>A 3.5% rise in price to the key $65,000 level could liquidate $1.36 billion in short positions, whereas a 3.5% drop to $60,500 would only wipe out $650 million in long positions.</p>



<p>In addition to the funding rate, traders are also monitoring the three-month annualised basis rate, which measures the cost difference between Bitcoin futures contracts and the actual price of Bitcoin.</p>



<p>Major exchanges have seen an increase in the annualised basis rate, reaching the higher end of the 5–10% neutral range. Traders often view rates above 10% as a neutral-to-bullish signal.</p>



<p>With both indicators showing signs of a potential bullish trend, traders are cautiously optimistic about Bitcoin&#8217;s price movement in the near term.</p>
