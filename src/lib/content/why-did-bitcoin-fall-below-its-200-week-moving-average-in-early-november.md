Bitcoin came under renewed pressure in early November, falling below its 200-week moving average as the U.S. dollar strengthened and global bond yields climbed. The decline pushed BTC to around $67,652, extending its monthly losses to approximately 16% and bringing a closely watched long-term technical level into focus.

The move coincided with weakness across several financial markets. Gold struggled to recover towards $4,000 an ounce, technology futures declined and oil prices rebounded. At the same time, Bitcoin's perpetual futures funding rate turned negative for the first time in a month, indicating a shift towards bearish positioning among derivatives traders.

The combination of macroeconomic pressure, technical weakness and changing derivatives sentiment offered a more complex picture than the price decline alone.

## Bitcoin breaks below a key long-term technical level

Bitcoin fell to approximately $67,652, down more than 3.5% over 24 hours and around 16% over the preceding month. In the process, it moved below its 200-week moving average, which stood near $68,000.

The 200-week moving average is a long-term technical indicator calculated from Bitcoin's average price over the previous 200 weeks. Traders often monitor it as a reference point for assessing the asset's broader market trend and historical price cycles.

A move below this level can indicate weakening market momentum and increase attention on further downside risks. However, the indicator does not establish a definitive support or resistance level, nor does a break below it determine how long a decline will continue.

The price movement therefore raised questions about whether Bitcoin was experiencing a temporary correction or entering a more prolonged period of weakness. The available price data alone could not resolve that question.

## A stronger dollar and rising bond yields add pressure

Bitcoin's decline took place alongside a broader shift in financial market conditions.

The U.S. Dollar Index (DXY) climbed above 103, while yields on government bonds increased across several major economies. The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield reached 5.125%, and the 30-year yield stood at 5.459%. Yields also moved higher in the United Kingdom, France, Germany and Japan.

Higher bond yields can make fixed-income assets more attractive relative to investments perceived as riskier. A stronger dollar can also weigh on assets priced in U.S. dollars by tightening financial conditions and changing investor demand.

These developments formed an unfavourable backdrop for cryptocurrencies. Bitcoin is often treated as a risk-sensitive asset, meaning its price can come under pressure when investors become more cautious or the cost of holding alternative investments increases.

Other markets showed signs of strain as well. Gold traded just below $3,900 an ounce while attempting to recover towards $4,000. Oil moved higher, with West Texas Intermediate crude near $89 a barrel and Brent above $96.

The simultaneous movements do not establish a single cause for Bitcoin's decline. They do, however, show that the cryptocurrency's weakness coincided with a wider repricing across financial markets.

## Negative funding signals a shift in derivatives positioning

Another important development was the change in Bitcoin perpetual futures funding.

According to Glassnode data cited in the market update, the average perpetual futures funding rate across exchanges turned negative for the first time in a month as Bitcoin traded around $68,500.

Funding rates are periodic payments exchanged between traders holding long and short positions in perpetual futures markets. They help keep the contract price aligned with the underlying asset's spot price.

When funding is positive, long-position holders generally pay short-position holders. Negative funding reverses this arrangement: traders holding short positions pay those holding long positions.

A negative rate therefore indicates that bearish positioning has become more pronounced relative to bullish positioning in the perpetual futures market.

This shift can have several implications. It may reflect growing expectations of further price declines, increased demand for short exposure or a reduction in leveraged long positions. In some market conditions, heavily negative funding can also coincide with local price bottoms, particularly if a subsequent price recovery forces short sellers to close positions.

However, negative funding is not a reliable reversal signal on its own. It describes the balance of positioning in a specific derivatives market, not the direction Bitcoin must take next.

For a clearer assessment, traders would need to examine funding alongside open interest, liquidation activity, spot trading volume and price behaviour around key technical levels.

## What the early-November decline revealed about Bitcoin's market position

The developments highlighted three factors that were shaping Bitcoin's market conditions.

First, the break below the 200-week moving average weakened the technical picture and placed a widely monitored long-term indicator under pressure.

Second, the stronger dollar and rising government bond yields provided a challenging macroeconomic backdrop for risk-sensitive assets. Bitcoin's decline was part of a broader period of market weakness rather than an isolated move.

Third, negative perpetual futures funding showed that derivatives positioning had turned more bearish. While this can sometimes precede a short-term recovery, it also reflects the degree of caution among traders at the time.

None of these signals independently determines Bitcoin's next move. Together, they provide a more useful framework for understanding the decline than focusing on a single price level or market indicator.

## What to watch after Bitcoin's move below $68,000

The next stage of Bitcoin's price movement will depend on whether the market can regain the area around its 200-week moving average and how broader financial conditions develop.

Several indicators are particularly relevant:

- The 200-week moving average: Whether Bitcoin can reclaim and hold this level may help assess whether the breakdown was temporary or part of a more sustained weakening trend.
- The U.S. Dollar Index and bond yields: Continued strength in the dollar and rising yields could maintain pressure on risk-sensitive assets.
- Perpetual futures funding: A return to positive funding may indicate improving derivatives sentiment, while persistently negative rates would suggest continued bearish positioning.
- Open interest and liquidation data: These figures can help show whether leverage is being removed from the market or whether traders are continuing to build directional positions.
- Spot market activity: Trading volume and demand in spot markets can help distinguish genuine buying interest from short-term movements driven primarily by derivatives.

The early-November decline did not provide a definitive answer about Bitcoin's longer-term direction. It did, however, expose the interaction between technical levels, macroeconomic conditions and derivatives positioning at a time when market sentiment was weakening.

For investors and market participants, monitoring these factors together offers a more complete view of Bitcoin's changing market structure than interpreting its price movement in isolation.
